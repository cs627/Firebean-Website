SECTION 02 — 市場洞察 / MARKET INSIGHT
Single-Message Strategy

The 8-Second Test: Why Government Communication Wins with One Message

Single-message government campaigns achieve 70% higher recall — multi-point campaigns lose most of their audience in 8 seconds.

The 8-Second Window

The average human attention span has dropped to 8 seconds — down from 12 seconds in 2000 (Microsoft Canada, 2015). In digital environments, 55% of visitors spend fewer than 15 seconds on any given page (Chartbeat, 2016). For government communicators, this means the window to land a public message is measured in heartbeats. When a department publishes three key announcements simultaneously, the public absorbs none of them.

One Message, 70% More Recall

Campaigns built around a single key message achieve 70% higher message recall than multi-message campaigns (Nielsen, 2024). The cognitive science is clear: working memory holds 3-4 items, but under time pressure it holds one. When a government campaign leads with one clear point — one benefit, one action, one deadline — the public remembers it. When it leads with five, they remember the font colour.

Focus Beats Volume

Single-focus public messaging drives 3x higher engagement compared to campaigns presenting multiple value propositions simultaneously (HubSpot, 2025). The UK Government Communication Service formally adopted a "one message, one audience" framework in 2022 after internal research showed multi-point campaigns lost 60% of intended recall. For Hong Kong's public sector, where citizens are already saturated with information across channels, the competitive advantage is not more content — it is one message, said clearly, repeated consistently. The Cision 2025 State of PR report found 89% of PR professionals cite content overload as their top challenge. The answer is not to add more noise. It is to cut through with a single, focused message that respects the audience's limited attention — and earns the 8 seconds you have.
