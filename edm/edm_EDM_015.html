SECTION 02 — 市場洞察 / MARKET INSIGHT
Event Efficacy

Crowd Management Is a Measurement Problem, Not a Logistics Problem

Your attendance figure is a liability. Your crowd density is a risk report. You get one news cycle — own all three before the next event.

Key Stat: 41%

The Audit Commission did not merely question government events — it sanctioned them. Its review of the Mega Events Fund found 69% of applications rejected, yet 41% of the events that WERE approved breached their funding terms and were financially sanctioned (Audit Commission Report No. 62). Organisers were never required to agree their counting method in advance: attendance data existed for crowd management, not performance evaluation. One event pledged 3,000 paid performers to LegCo; auditors found 1,317 paid jobs, 410 of them primary-school and kindergarten “performers”. An unverified attendance number is not evidence — it is exposure.

Key Stat: 6

The turnout you celebrate in a press release is the same number a safety engineer reads as a density calculation. Above roughly 6 people per square metre crowds lose control — people are lifted off their feet or fall, and the disaster that follows is almost always organisational, not malicious. Hong Kong already knows this: nearly 80,000 people were drawn to Lan Kwai Fong for Halloween, requiring a dedicated Police crowd-management operation, and Kai Tak Sports Park ran around 20 test events and five large-scale drills involving nearly 140,000 civil servants before a single gate opened. Efficacy and safety are not competing KPIs — a crowd you cannot read is a crowd you cannot keep.

Key Stat: 44%

You spend public money to be seen — then compete for seconds in a feed where 44% of 18-24 year-olds say social media, not a newsroom, is their main source of news (Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2025). Global trust in news sits at 40%; only 35% check a doubtful claim against an official source; 65% consume news via social video, up from 52% in 2020; and roughly four in ten actively avoid news. Meanwhile about half of journalists told Cision they received pitches they wanted to cover but could not, because time and newsroom resources were gone. The press release is not dead — but if you do not own the narrative through your own channels, video and post-event data, the only coverage that reliably breaks through is the coverage you cannot control. Behind all three numbers is one pattern: government events are judged on evidence, not effort. Firebean builds the measurement method, the crowd plan and the narrative into a single brief — so the story you own after the event is the story you chose before it.
 
SECTION 03 — / CASE STUDY
GOVERNMENTNarcotics Division, Security Bureau (保安局禁毒處)

Crowd Management at Scale: Firebean at the 34th Hong Kong Book Fair

How Firebean kept queues flowing and the visitor experience congestion-free at one of Hong Kong's highest-traffic public exhibitions.

The Narcotics Division of the Security Bureau needed more than visibility at the 34th Hong Kong Book Fair — it needed an anti-drug message that reached families and young people inside one of the city's busiest halls without turning the zone into a bottleneck. Firebean delivered concept development, theme and visual identity, UI/UX, interactive installation, video and motion graphics, talent and RSVP management, on-site operation and technical support. The real constraint was crowd flow: with continuous waves of visitors moving through the Children's Paradise zone, the team designed the space for movement — roaming staff with giveaways paced visitors through the area, interactive “Anti-drug Info Zone” mechanics held attention long enough to clear the aisles, and queues were managed so they kept flowing instead of jamming. The outcome was a public-health message delivered at scale in a space that stayed congestion-free — proof that crowd management is not crowd control: it is the craft that lets a message land with thousands of people at once.
