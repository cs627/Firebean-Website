Museum FestivalLeisure and Cultural Services Department (LCSD)

Muse Fest HK 2026 opens with a pixel-art carnival at the Cultural Centre Piazza

Muse Fest HK 2026 opens with the “Fun@Museum Carnival” on 31 October and 1 November, 11am–6pm daily, at the Hong Kong Cultural Centre Piazza in Tsim Sha Tsui. This year’s theme, “Collage the Past, Hong Kong Story”, translates old Hong Kong — cha chaan tengs, Chinese medicine halls, coin-toss arcades — into a pixelated visual language, with three large installations, five “Memory Unboxed” workshops and a 1m × 1.5m giant pixel egg tart that visitors complete together. All activities are free. It opens a month-long programme across 17 museums — a case where heritage storytelling and public footfall have to be designed together.

Mega EventHong Kong Tourism Board (HKTB)

Hong Kong Wine & Dine Festival 2026 returns to the Central Harbourfront

Organised by the Hong Kong Tourism Board with the Bordeaux Wine Council, the Hong Kong Wine & Dine Festival 2026 runs four days, 29 October to 1 November, across the Central Harbourfront Event Space and Tamar Park under the theme “Savour the World’s Best”. More than 340 booths from around 31 countries and regions — over 10 per cent more than last year — pair global wines with Hong Kong hotel and Michelin kitchens. Entry runs through Trip.com, with tasting passes from HK$258. Across a four-day, multi-zone outdoor site, queue design and visitor-flow control shape the experience as much as the wine list does.

SECTION 02 — 市場洞察 / MARKET INSIGHT
Digital Heritage

Digital Twins: The Invitation, Not the Destination

Digital twins can put a museum in everyone's pocket. The visit is still the point — no online experience has ever replaced standing in the room.

Digital Twin Market, 2025

The raw material for digital engagement is no longer scarce. AI-generated imagery, film and 3D capture are mature enough that any museum can produce an unlimited stream of visual assets, and the tools behind them are large business: MarketsandMarkets puts the global digital twin market at US$21.14 billion in 2025, forecast to reach US$149.81 billion by 2030. That matters for one reason — digital has become a credible touchpoint and promotional channel, the cheapest way to be in someone's hand before they decide to come.

Visits to Digital Dunhuang

The audience for that channel is already large. The Dunhuang Academy's Digital Dunhuang portal for the Mogao Caves has recorded over 47 million visits — for a UNESCO World Heritage site that most of those visitors will never reach in person. Read honestly, this is a promotional number, not an attendance number: it measures interest created, not experience delivered. Every museum that puts itself online is doing it to pull people through the door.

Engaged With Heritage Online

That distinction is where most programmes go wrong. In England, the DCMS Participation Survey 2024–25 cited in Historic England's Heritage Counts found 24% of adults engaged with heritage digitally, against 66% who visited a historic site in person — digital engagement runs at roughly one-third of physical engagement. What actually moves a person is still standing in the room: the light, the scale, the object at arm's length. A digital twin can raise the interactivity of that visit. It cannot be the visit. For Hong Kong the design brief follows: build the digital layer as a funnel with an exit — a timed entry, a guided walk, a programmed room with a person in it. Muse Fest HK 2026 brings the Fun@Museum Carnival to the Cultural Centre Piazza and the Wine & Dine Festival takes over the Central Harbourfront; the test afterwards is not how many watched the feed, but how many turned up. Digital earns its budget as the invitation and the interactivity, never as the substitute.
